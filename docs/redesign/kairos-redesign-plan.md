# Kairos Website Redesign — Three Directions to Choose From

This is a plain-language walkthrough of what's been built for you to look at, why it's built this way, and what we need from you next. Once this is deployed to a preview link, you can click through all of it on your phone.

## The short version

The old Wix site read like a brochure — here's what we offer, here's our hours, here's a map. It told visitors *about* Kairos. What it never did was tell them *why they should call today*.

Everything below is built around a different idea: every page should walk a worried parent through **the problem they're actually feeling → Kairos as the guide who's done this before → one clear next step → what life looks like after.** That's the "StoryBrand" framework (Donald Miller) — your family is the hero of the story, Kairos is the guide, not the hero. Layered on top is a "get to the phone call" mindset (Alex Hormozi's lead-gen approach) — every page keeps pointing at one action, because a homepage that offers ten things to click usually gets zero of them clicked.

## Three homepage directions

Three full comps live at `/concepts/a`, `/concepts/b`, `/concepts/c` (linked from `/concepts`). Pick the one that feels most like Kairos — or tell us which pieces of each you like and we'll combine them.

- **Concept A — The Direct Guide.** The most "sales page"-feeling of the three. Names the problem plainly in the headline, repeats one call-to-action ("Book a free call") several times down the page, and keeps the visual noise low. Best if you want the homepage to convert as hard as possible, closest to what Hormozi-style landing pages look like.
- **Concept B — A Day at Kairos.** Leads with a full-bleed photo and a softer, story-driven scroll — feels more like a "who we are" experience before it asks for anything. Best if you want the homepage to feel warm and editorial first, sales-y second. This is the one that will benefit most from your new photo-day pictures.
- **Concept C — Find Your Path.** Structured around "which program fits your family" — good for visitors who found you through Google search already comparing tutoring options, since it helps them self-sort into Private Tutoring, Homework Club, Homeschool Support, or APEX before asking for a call.

**None of these are live yet.** They're marked so search engines won't index them, and nothing links to them from the real site's menu — they only exist at their direct links until you pick one.

## The two ad campaigns

For Facebook ads, there are dedicated landing pages — no site menu, nothing to click except "call now" or a two-field "call me back" form (just name and phone, on purpose — Hormozi's approach favors getting someone on the phone fast over a long form that people abandon).

- APEX: `/lp/apex` (no menu — use this one for ads) and `/lp/apex-site` (same content, with the normal site menu — for comparison, or if you'd rather ads land with the menu present)
- Private Tutoring: `/lp/tutoring` (no menu) and `/lp/tutoring-site` (with menu)

Whichever pair you like better, that's the one whose URL goes into the actual Facebook ad.

## What this does NOT include yet (on purpose)

- The interior pages (About, Services, Testimonials, Fall Classes, Summer) haven't been touched — once you pick a homepage direction, we'll bring the rest of the site in line with it as a follow-up, so you're not reviewing 15 pages at once.
- Real photos aren't in yet — the comps use the 3 existing site photos as placeholders so you can judge layout and story, not final imagery.
- No ad tracking (Facebook Pixel, conversion tracking) is wired up yet — that's a fast follow once you're actually running ads.
- **Font is an open question.** The current site uses Fraunces (headlines) and Inter (body text) — both stay as-is in these comps. Once you pick a homepage direction, that's a good time to also decide if the typography should change to match.
